## A Prayer Before We Begin

Before Scripture speaks, we pray. Let us ask God to do what only He can do.

> Father, we come to this moment knowing that we are weak interpreters of a strong Word. I ask that You would silence my own rhetoric. Let the Scripture I bring be heard as *Your* voice, not mine. I have listened to Eric's faith articulated over many hours. Now let him listen to what You have said. Open his heart not to *my* persuasion, but to the *authority of Your Word*. If there is false teaching here, let it be exposed by Scripture, not by my argument. If there is repentance to come, let it be to *You*, not to me. And let the end of this be not that Eric agrees with me, but that Eric sees Christ as He is revealed in His Word. In Christ's name. Amen.

## A Heart Broken Over What I Heard

I have been praying for you all week. Night after night, I have been awakened or unable to sleep. This is not out of anger or anxiety, this is genuine concern.

I want to be brutally honest as you have asked me to be: my heart is broken by what you have shown me about your faith. I am deeply grieved by it. When you asked me about the sermon on Sunday, you said it was about sin, and my heart broke open. What I heard on Sunday was the richness and the revelation of Christ in Scripture. And then I heard your response to it. And I had to ask: *What does Eric truly believe? What does he put his faith in?*

I have spent three years with you, Eric. I know your heart. I know your commitment. But I did not know what you believe and where you place your faith until now. So asking that question enlightened me, and revealed a dark place that you have been living in for thirty years.

## What Eight Hours of Listening Revealed

I have listened to you speak for over eight hours: two Saturdays, half an hour Sunday, an hour and a half on the phone. I was saddened by what I heard. Disappointed. And grieved—because false teaching, I'm sorry to say, is what this has become.

I know these are harsh words. I do not say them lightly. But I am asking for this opportunity to share what has been laid on my heart, and I ask that you *listen without interruption*. 

Now during our meetings we have not once cracked open a Bible, so everything we have said has been vain and produced nothing but opinions and argument.

That is going to stop today, because that should be our foundation. This can only be productive if it is based on Scripture. And Scripture is what I am bringing to you now.

You spoke of how believers were always striving to be more holy and how they told others to do the same. So I have taken the liberty to be your scribe and write out your own translation. ## Reading the Actual Translation

### Matthew 5:17–20 (NASB1995)

> **Matthew 5:17–20 (NASB1995)**
>
> *17 "Do not think that I came to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I did not come to abolish but to fulfill. 18 For truly I say to you, until heaven and earth pass away, not the smallest letter or stroke shall pass from the Law until all is accomplished. 19 Whoever then annuls one of the least of these commandments, and teaches others to do the same, shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven; but whoever keeps and teaches them, he shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven. 20 For I say to you that unless your righteousness surpasses that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven."*

**What was removed:** The word *"fulfill."* Your translation reads as if Jesus came to enforce the Law through you, to make you prove yourself against it. The actual text says He came to *complete* it. He satisfied its demands. He bore its penalty. The Law points to what He did.

**What was replaced:** The subject of the sentence. In EMLT, the subject becomes *you*—"I came so I can show Eric the Law." In the actual text, the subject remains *Christ*—"I came to fulfill." The righteousness that matters is not something you achieve. It is something that has been accomplished and can be imputed to you.

### Psalm 139:1–24 (NASB1995)

> **Psalm 139 (NASB1995)**
>
> *1 O Lord, You have searched me and known me. 2 You know when I sit down and when I rise up; You understand my thought from afar. 3 You scrutinize my going out and my lying down, And are intimately acquainted with all my ways. 4 Even before there is a word on my tongue, Behold, O Lord, You know it all. 5 You have enclosed me behind and before, And laid Your hand upon me. 6 Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; It is too high, I cannot attain to it.*
>
> *7 Where can I go from Your Spirit? Or where can I flee from Your presence? 8 If I ascend to heaven, You are there; If I make my bed in Sheol, behold, You are there. 9 If I take the wings of the dawn, If I dwell in the remotest part of the sea, 10 Even there Your hand will lead me, And Your right hand will lay hold of me. 11 If I say, "Surely the darkness will overwhelm me, And the light around me will be night," 12 Even the darkness is not dark to You, And the night is as bright as the day. Darkness and light are alike to You.*
>
> *13 For You formed my inward parts; You wove me in my mother's womb. 14 I will give thanks to You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made; Wonderful are Your works, And my soul knows it very well. 15 My frame was not hidden from You, When I was made in secret, And skillfully wrought in the depths of the earth; 16 Your eyes have seen my unformed substance; And in Your book were all written The days that were ordained for me, When as yet there was not one of them.*
>
> *17 How precious also are Your thoughts to me, O God! How vast is the sum of them! 18 If I should count them, they would outnumber the sand. When I awake, I am still with You. 19 O that You would slay the wicked, O God; Depart from me, therefore, men of bloodshed. 20 For they say of You, "Those who speak of You, speak evil." 21 Do I not hate those who hate You, O Lord? And do I not loathe those who rise up against You? 22 I hate them with the utmost hatred; They have become my enemies.*
>
> *23 Search me, O God, and know my heart; Try me and know my anxious thoughts; 24 And see if there be any hurtful way in me, And lead me in the everlasting way.*

**What the psalm actually means:**

Verses 1–22 establish a single, overwhelming truth: God knows everything about you. Not in a general sense—*everything*. Your sitting and rising. Your thoughts from afar. Your ways. Words before you speak them. He is inescapably present. There is nowhere to run. He knit you in the womb. He has numbered your days. And His thoughts toward you are precious beyond counting.

This is not preparation for self-examination. This is *the foundation for rest*. 

Only *after* establishing that God already knows all things does the psalmist pray in verses 23–24: *"Search me, O God, and know my heart."* But he is not asking God to *discover* something hidden. God already knows. The psalmist is *inviting* examination, *yielding* to it—resting in the fact that an omniscient God sees him completely and loves him still.

And the prayer ends not with "show me my sin so I can fix it," but with *"lead me in the everlasting way."* The goal is not introspection. The goal is *direction*.

## What Matthew 5:17–20 Actually Says

Jesus made a statement so absolute that it should stop every hearer cold: *"Unless your righteousness surpasses that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven."*

This was not a challenge to try harder. It was an indictment of the entire category of human righteousness.

The scribes and Pharisees were the *most righteous people of their day by any measurable standard*. They kept the Law meticulously. They added hedge-laws to protect the Law. They fasted, prayed, tithed, memorized Scripture. They were the gold standard of external righteousness.

And Jesus said: *Your righteousness must surpass theirs or you will not enter the kingdom.*

**Why would He say this?** Because it is impossible. No human righteousness can surpass theirs—they are the best of human effort. The statement is an indictment of the entire enterprise of earning righteousness.

Then Jesus explains why: He came not to abolish the Law but to *fulfill it*. He, Jesus, kept the Law perfectly. He satisfied its demands. He bore its penalty. He is the righteousness that surpasses the Pharisees—not because He tried harder, but because He is the Son of God.

The message is not: *"Work yourself into righteousness you don't have."*

The message is: *"The righteousness you need has come. It is not from you. It is from Me."*

## What Psalm 139 Actually Means

Psalm 139 is a declaration of God's omniscience and omnipresence. It is not a manual for psychological self-examination.

Verses 1–12 establish the foundation: God knows everything. God is everywhere. There is nowhere to flee from His presence. He knows your thoughts before you speak them. This is not a question or an invitation—it is a statement of fact.

Then, in verses 23–24, the psalmist prays: *"Search me, O God, and know my heart."*

But notice—*God already knows*. The psalmist is not asking God to discover something hidden. The psalmist is *inviting* God's examination, resting in the fact that God already sees everything. And the prayer concludes: *"lead me in the everlasting way."*

The goal is not endless introspection. The goal is *God's leading*.

**What Eric's Miserable Living Translation does** is turn this into: *"God, show me every hidden sin. Unearth my darkness. Reveal what lies in the depths so I can find it and fix it."*

But that is not what the psalm says. That is Eric forcing the text into the grid of works righteousness. The psalm says: *God, You see everything about me already. Search me—examine me—and then lead me in Your way. I trust Your knowledge and Your direction.*

It is an act of resting in God's omniscience, not a permission slip for morbid introspection.

---

## The Trap: Righteousness as Achievement

This is the pattern: take a passage that announces grace, read it through the lens of self-improvement, and transform it into a works project.

Matthew 5:20 becomes: *"I must achieve a righteousness greater than the greatest."*

And in both cases, the focus narrows: from Christ to self. From what God has done to what Eric must do. From grace to effort.

This is the opposite of what both passages teach.

Matthew 5:17 announces that Christ came to *fulfill* the Law—not to demand a greater Law from those who follow. The righteousness that matters is His, imputed to those who believe.

Psalm 139 rests in God's complete knowledge and trustworthy leading—not in human effort to lay bare the self.

## The Problem of Thirty Years and Unanswered Questions

There is a subtle pride that can grow over decades of faith. The assumption: *I have been walking with Christ for thirty years. I have read the Bible through many times. I have done the work. Surely I understand the foundations.*

But then a simple question arrives: *Who is Jesus?*

And the response is deflection. *Why would you ask me that? That is for new believers.*

This is the moment to ask: *Is the foundation so secure that it no longer needs to be named? Or is it that naming it would expose something?*

Consider this: when a believer has been engaged in works righteousness for decades, the answer to "Who is Jesus?" often comes out as *"He is the One who requires my obedience. He is the One whose will I must discover. He is the One whose depths I must plumb."*

But that is not who He is.

Jesus is the One who came to do what no human can do. He is the fulfiller of the Law. He is the One whose glory believers are invited to behold. He is the One whose righteousness is imputed—not earned.

The deflection of the simple question reveals the trapped foundation.

## The Severity of Trampling What Christ Did

> **Hebrews 10:26–29 (NASB1995)**
>
> *"For if we go on sinning willfully after receiving the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a terrifying expectation of judgment and the fury of a fire which will consume the adversaries. Anyone who has rejected the Law of Moses dies without mercy on the basis of two or three witnesses. How much severer punishment do you think he will deserve who has trampled under foot the Son of God, and has regarded as unclean the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified, and has insulted the Spirit of grace?"*

This passage describes two categories of judgment. The first is willful sin—persistent, knowing rebellion. That carries judgment.

But the second is far darker: it is to *trample the Son of God underfoot*. To regard His atoning blood as *unclean*. To *insult the Spirit of grace*.

This is not the language of innocence. This is contempt.

A man who has walked with Christ for thirty years, who has been taught the sufficiency of Christ's sacrifice, who knows that Christ bore the penalty of sin and satisfied divine justice—yet continues to build a life around the notion that this is *not enough*. That he must search deeper into his sin. That he must ascend higher in personal righteousness. That Christ's work requires his completion.

That is saying, in effect: *Your blood is unclean. Your sacrifice is incomplete. Your work did not finish what needed to be done.*

It is not an intellectual disagreement about hermeneutics. It is the trampling underfoot of what Christ accomplished.

Hebrews does not say such contempt will go unanswered. It promises *much severer punishment*—not for the confused, but for those who knew better and built their lives in defiance of that knowledge.

The question for Eric is not whether he has erred in his exegesis. The question is whether he is prepared to stop trampling the grace that saved him, and receive it as sufficient.

## Basic Questions of Faith

I have asked you one fundamental question: *Who is Jesus?*

Your response was defensive. You said this is a question for new believers, not for someone who has walked thirty years. And then I understood something: the defensiveness itself reveals the problem.

A mature Christian does not flinch from the foundational question. He answers it clearly. Because the foundation is what everything rests on.

Before we continue, I need to address the defensiveness directly. I am not asking this question to shame you or to suggest you are immature. I am asking because your framework—the one we have examined in Matthew 5, Psalm 139, and Hebrews 10—does not comport with a clear answer to this question.

I am asking you to answer without defensiveness. Not as a performance. Not as a proof of your credentials. Simply: *Who do you say that Jesus is?*

And I need to ask you two more foundational questions.

### Question 1: Who Do You Say That I Am?

> **Matthew 16:15–17 (NASB1995)**
>
> *"He said to them, 'But who do you say that I am?' Simon Peter answered, 'You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.' And Jesus said to him, 'Blessed are you, Simon Barjona, because flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but My Father who is in heaven.'"*

**The rule:** Answer this question. But if your answer contains the words *I*, *me*, or *my*—your answer does not qualify. The answer is not about you. It is about who Christ is and what He claims.

Examples that disqualify: "He is the One I follow," "He means everything to me," "He is what my soul needs."

Examples that qualify: "He is the Son of God," "He is the One who fulfilled all righteousness," "He is the sacrifice for sins."

### Question 2: What's the Password to Get Into Heaven?

I have an article for you on this topic. We will read it together. The question is simple: What do you say someone must do—or be—to enter heaven?

**The rule:** Answer this question. But if your answer contains the words *I*, *me*, or *my*—your answer does not qualify. This is not about your personal journey. It is about what the Bible says.

Examples that disqualify: "I must repent," "My faith opens the door," "I need to be holy."

Examples that qualify: "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ," "Trust in His finished work," "His righteousness is imputed to me."

### Homework Assignment

Write out your answers to both questions:
1. Who do you say that Jesus is?
2. What's the password to get into heaven?

Do not use the words *I*, *me*, or *my* in either answer. Write them out—do not give them verbally. This forces clarity. It prevents the habit of vagueness and deflection that comes easily in conversation.

Bring these answers back to our next meeting. We will discuss them then.

## You Say That You Are a Mature Christian (30 Years)

Scripture speaks to this claim. Let us read what the writer of Hebrews says about maturity in Christ.

> **Hebrews 5:11–6:8 (NASB1995)**
>
> *Concerning him we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing. 12 For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you have need again for someone to teach you the elementary principles of the oracles of God, and you have come to need milk and not solid food. 13 For everyone who partakes only of milk is not accustomed to the word of righteousness, for he is an infant. 14 But solid food is for the mature, who because of practice have their senses trained to discern good and evil.*
>
> *Therefore leaving the elementary teaching about the Christ, let us press on to maturity, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, 2 of instruction about washings and laying on of hands, and the resurrection of the dead and eternal judgment. 3 And this we will do, if God permits. 4 For in the case of those who have once been enlightened and have tasted of the heavenly gift and have been made partakers of the Holy Spirit, 5 and have tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come, 6 and then have fallen away, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance, since they again crucify to themselves the Son of God and put Him to open shame. 7 For ground that drinks the rain which often falls on it and brings forth vegetation useful to those for whose sake it is also tilled, receives a blessing from God; 8 but if it yields thorns and thistles, it is rejected and close to being cursed, whose end is to be burned.*

**What the writer of Hebrews diagnoses:**

These are believers who have been taught. They have tasted grace. They have received the Holy Spirit. They know the foundational truths. And yet they are still infants, still on milk, still unable to move from the basics.

The writer is not gentle about this. He says they "have become dull of hearing." They *ought* to be teachers by now, but instead they need someone to teach them again what they should already know.

And then comes the warning: if someone has been enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, received the Holy Spirit, tasted God's Word and the powers of the age to come—*and then falls away*—it is "impossible to renew them again to repentance."

Why? Because to fall away after such enlightenment is to "crucify to themselves the Son of God and put Him to open shame."

**This is precisely what happened to Eric.**

Thirty years in the faith. You have been taught repentance, faith, grace, the sufficiency of Christ. And yet you remain on the foundation—not building on it, but digging beneath it, questioning it, treating it as insufficient. Your framework does not build. It dismantles.

You have tasted the heavenly gift. And you have fallen away into works.

## What Christ's Desire Actually Is

In John 17:24, Jesus prays to the Father. This is not a prayer for instruction on self-improvement. This is not a prayer asking the Father to reveal your hidden sins. This is the High Priestly Prayer, and here is what Jesus desires:

> *"Father, I desire that they also, whom You have given Me, be with Me where I am, so that they may see My glory which You have given Me, for You loved Me before the foundation of the world."*

**This is what Christ desires for you:**
- To be **with Him**
- To **see His glory**
- To rest in the love that existed before the foundation of the world

Not: *endless self-examination*
Not: *constant searching into hidden sin*
Not: *an ever-escalating project of self-improvement*
Not: *trying to surpass the Pharisees in righteousness*

His desire is **intimacy with Himself and the vision of His glory**.

**Here is the hard truth:** If you are willing to do Christ's desire, then His desire should be yours. And when your focus shifts from your sin to His glory, your sin awareness will no longer be your foundation.

You have spent thirty years asking: *What is wrong with me? What sin am I missing? How can I become more righteous?*

Christ asks something else entirely: *Will you be with Me? Will you see My glory? Will you rest in the love I have for you?*

That is not a question you can answer while you are still digging into yourself. It is a question that requires you to look away from the self entirely and fix your gaze on Him.

## The Invitation

Eric has been given a choice between two translations of Scripture. 

One is the **Eric's Miserable Living Translation**: works-righteousness filtered through thirty years of self-focus, turning passages about God's knowledge and Christ's sufficiency into demands for endless introspection and moral achievement.

The other is the **actual text**: Matthew 5:17–20 announcing that Christ fulfilled what no human can. Psalm 139:23–24 resting in God's complete knowledge and trustworthy leading. John 17:24 revealing Christ's actual desire: that you be with Him and behold His glory.

The move from one translation to the other requires a choice. It requires abandoning the grid of self-righteousness and accepting the message of grace. It requires answering the foundational questions—*Who is Jesus? What is the password to heaven?*—not by reference to thirty years of your own experience, but by reference to what Scripture reveals.

It requires looking away from the self and gazing at Christ's glory.

**The time for that choice is now.**
