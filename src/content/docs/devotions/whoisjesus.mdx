## Read: Matthew 16:13–28

> *13 Now when Jesus came into the district of Caesarea Philippi, He asked His disciples, saying, "Who do people say that the Son of Man is?" 14 And they said, "Some say John the Baptist; and others, Elijah; but still others, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets." 15 He said to them, "But who do you say that I am?" 16 Simon Peter answered, "You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God." 17 And Jesus said to him, "Blessed are you, Simon Barjona, because flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but My Father who is in heaven."*

---

## The Question That Divides Everything

Jesus stood among His disciples at Caesarea Philippi and asked a question that sounds simple but is not. It was not asked as though He did not know the answer. He knew. He knew who He was. But He asked it anyway, and He asked it directly: "Who do you say that I am?"

This was not a theological quiz. This was a threshold question — the kind that determines whether someone stands inside or outside the kingdom of heaven. The answer to this single question shapes every other answer: the purpose of Scripture, the nature of sin, the work of redemption, the structure of eternity, the call on a life.

In Matthew 16:18, Jesus would say to Peter, "On this rock I will build My church." But the "rock" was not Peter himself. It was Peter's confession — the revealed knowledge that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of the living God. The Church stands on the answer to that question.

---

## The Completeness of Peter's Answer—and Its Incompleteness

Peter answered correctly. He said, "You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God." Jesus affirmed him. The Father had revealed this truth to him. Peter's confession was not flesh and blood reasoning; it was truth revealed from heaven.

Yet Peter did not fully understand what he had confessed.

Three chapters later, in Matthew 19:27–29, Peter would still be asking about rewards and thrones. Weeks later, Peter would deny even knowing Jesus. Months later, in Acts 10, Peter would struggle with the gospel going to the Gentiles. Peter knew who Jesus was — the Christ, the Son of God — but he did not yet understand what that meant, nor had he grasped the full dimensions of Christ's person and work.

This is crucial: a believer can answer the cornerstone question correctly and still be growing toward understanding. The question "Who is Jesus?" is not one answered once and set aside. It deepens.

---

## What Scripture Reveals: The Scope of His Identity

Scripture peels back layers of who Jesus is. Each revelation is truth that stands eternally, waiting to be understood more completely.

He is the **Eternal Word and Creator**. *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things came into being through Him"* **(John 1:1–3)**. Not made. Not created. Existing eternally with God the Father, yet distinct in person. And through Him — all things.

He is the **Radiance of God's Glory**. *"He is the radiance of His glory and the exact representation of His nature, and He upholds all things by the word of His power"* **(Hebrews 1:3)**. When one looks upon Jesus, one sees God Himself—not a reflection, not a representation, but the very radiance of God's glory made visible in human form.

He is the **Incarnate God** who took on flesh. *"And the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us"* **(John 1:14)**. The eternal Son of God, the Creator of all things, entered into His own creation. He was born. He grew. He ate. He slept. He wept. And He remained fully God while being fully human.

He is the **Sinless Sacrifice**. *"By this will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all"* **(Hebrews 10:10)**. He lived a sinless life in the place of sinners and *"bore our sins in His body on the cross"* **(1 Peter 2:24 NASB1995)**. His death was not a tragedy that happened to Him. It was the purpose for which He came.

He is the **Risen Lord** who conquered death. *"Now Christ has been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep"* **(1 Corinthians 15:20)**. The grave could not hold Him. Death could not contain the Author of Life. He rose bodily, appeared to witnesses, and proved Himself alive.

He is the **Exalted King** seated at the right hand of the Father. *"He sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high"* **(Hebrews 1:3)** and *"all authority has been given to Him, both in heaven and on earth"* **(Matthew 28:18)**. He is not merely a historical figure or a moral teacher. He is the sovereign Lord of all creation, ruling now and forevermore.

He is the **Coming Judge** who will return. *"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each one may receive compensation for his deeds done through the body, in accordance with what he has done, whether good or bad"* **(2 Corinthians 5:10)**. Every life will face His evaluation. Every deed will be brought to light.

---

## Why Maturity Does Not Bypass This Question

A certain assumption exists: the longer one follows Christ, the more one moves beyond foundational questions. The thinking goes that foundational truths are for new believers, while mature believers move on to deeper things.

This is backward.

The longer one walks with Christ, the more the answer to "Who is Jesus?" should deepen. A mature believer should be able to articulate not merely "Jesus is my Lord and Savior" — though that is true — but to speak with theological precision about His eternal nature, His substitutionary atonement, His sovereignty over all things, His resurrection power, and His certain return. A mature believer should be able to distinguish between the person of Christ and the work of Christ, between His roles as Prophet, Priest, and King, between His first advent and His second advent.

The tragedy is when spiritual maturity becomes an excuse to stop asking foundational questions. When the answer is assumed rather than renewed. When "Who is Jesus?" is treated as settled and set aside.

But consider: if one's understanding of Jesus shifts, everything shifts. If one begins to soften His deity, grace changes. If one minimizes His substitutionary atonement, the cross itself changes. If one flattens His sovereignty, prayer changes. If one delays His return, urgency changes.

The question does not become less important because one has been walking with Christ for thirty years. It becomes more important.

---

## The Question Posed

This is where the conversation turns inward. Not to feeling, but to confession. Not to opinion, but to truth.

The disciples were asked. Peter was asked. The Pharisees, the rich young ruler, the Samaritan woman at the well — all were asked directly or confronted with the reality of who Jesus claimed to be.

Now the question arrives here:

**Who is Jesus?**

Not who does the culture say He is. Not who has He been in the past. Not who do others claim He is. But who does Jesus reveal Himself to be when Scripture is read carefully, when the historical record is honored, when His own words are taken at face value?

The answer will reveal everything about how one understands God, salvation, sin, holiness, judgment, grace, and eternity. The answer will show whether one stands on the rock or on sand.

The answer cannot hide in personal experience or private feeling. It must be stated. It must be defended. It must be lived out.

**Who is Jesus?**

Let that question sit. Let it be answered not in haste but in the presence of Scripture and the work of the Spirit. And let the answer be one that would satisfy not new believers testing the waters, but a disciple who has counted the cost and chosen to follow.
